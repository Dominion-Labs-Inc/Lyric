Taught a LIVE substrate (`start()`, coordination cycle and reactive drain worker running), so what the
learning authority announced was heard and reacted to.

WRITTEN BY HAND, BECAUSE THE PASS DID NOT WRITE ITS OWN. The pass was stopped in its final phase, before
it reached the point where it writes this file, so every figure here was read from the store afterwards or
from the pass's own progress display. What it taught was already committed: a word is recorded as it is
read, not at the end.

## The substrate

The 67,428 new records are the 67,427 words measured as having none, and one more. Read back through the

words hold one from the treebanks and the Parallel Meaning Bank, which name words WordNet does not.

## Why it was stopped

The machine ran out of memory. The pass grew from 8.1 GB resident to 14.5 GB, swap filled to 6,089 MB of
7,168 MB, and free pages fell to about 24 MB, at which point the last 503 words of the display took 25
minutes against the 90 seconds a thousand had been taking. Postgres serves this store and the production
side-stores from the same machine, so the pass was sent SIGTERM rather than left to page. It exited, and
free pages recovered to 16.7 GB.

Nothing was lost by stopping it. What it cost was the review pass, in which the substrate asks itself
what it has learned, and the automatic writing of this record.

## What this does not cover

A word's FORMS are not WordNet's to give. `word_record_of` takes them from the English Web Treebank and
GUM, so a word carries the forms English gives it only where those corpora wrote it in them. Regular
inflection across the open vocabulary has no source here, and recording every word has not changed that.

## What was measured wrong first

The cost of this pass was quoted three times before it was run: 605ms a word from an old note, then
1,497ms from timing a record built on a sample drawn across the whole vocabulary, then 252ms from the
difference between a 25-word pass and a 125-word one. The last was right, and was the one set aside. The
pass did 236ms a word.
