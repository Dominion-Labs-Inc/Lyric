# GRAMMAR-01, 2026-11-03 — what a wider chart buys, and what the held-out errors are

Files only: the grammar built from `data/lessons/english_[0-9][0-9].json` and PMB 5.1.0 gold train, read over 400
sentences of `dev/standard` (seed 1). No store, no memory, reads only. Build 575 s.

## A wider chart

`Grammar.charts(tokens, how_many)` keeps `how_many` readings of each category of each stretch instead of one, and
`Grammar.readings` hands the whole readings up for a listener to choose between
(`experiments/GRAMMAR-01/diagnostics/nbest.py`).

| readings kept of each category | the reader's own choice is right | the right reading is among those handed up | meanings handed up | s/sentence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| 50 | 52.7% | **58.7%** | 6.4 | 2.11 |

**It does not plateau.** The exhaustive chart holds the right reading for 61.8% of these sentences
(`every_reading.py`, entry 108), and a beam of fifty reaches 58.7% — about two thirds of the headroom between the
reader's own choice and the oracle, **+6.0 points**, the largest lever measured on the reader to date. It is not
free: fourteen times the reader's time against a beam of six, 2.11 s a sentence.

Two things bound what it can become. **The reader alone gains nothing**: its own choice is 79 of 150 at every width
from six to fifty, flat, so a wider search is worth exactly what a chooser can make of it and nothing by itself (the
54.2% → 54.0% seen over 400 sentences at widths three and four is one sentence, not a trend). And **the right
reading sits at the top of the list when it appears**: at fifty, of the nine newly available, seven rank first and
one each second and fifth. A chooser needs to weigh a handful of readings, not a haystack.

`COMPOSED_READINGS` is 1 until a chooser is measured, because what the listener would **select** among these is
untested: it weighs what memory knows of what each reading says, and this run has no memory (files only). That
measurement needs a store.

## Every held-out error, by the layer that must change

The 187 sentences not read right, split by whether the reader named the wrong things or named the right things and
joined them wrong.

| | of the 187 | of all 400 |
|---|---:|---:|
| the right things named, JOINED WRONG | 102 (54.5%) | **25.5%** |
| things named that are not there | 49 (26.2%) | 12.2% |
| some named that are not there, some there not named | 18 (9.6%) | 4.5% |
| things there it never named | 6 (3.2%) | 1.5% |
| said nothing at all | 12 (6.4%) | 3.0% |

The 94 where no reading joins the right things, by the one general difference that would make the reading the gold
(free variables aligned to maximise overlap first):

| | |
|---|---:|
| the links themselves differ | 68 (72.3%) |
| the same links, joined to other things: attachment | 13 (13.8%) |
| whether a concept is named or made an instance: the level | 11 (11.7%) |
| which facts carry `not`: the negation's scope | 2 (2.1%) |

**Three candidate repairs refuted here.** Verb valency (entry 108: the gold category is available for 95.8% of
aligned words); the kind-versus-particular level (4 of 94 in that direction, 11 both ways); the negation's scope
(2 of 94). `A spider has legs.` reading `owns` over instances where the gold says `has_part(spider, leg)` is a
lesson-set shape and not the held-out failure mode.

## The families inside the 18.2 points of words named wrong

| family | sentences | e.g. |
|---|---:|---|
| a nationality adjective read as the inhabitant noun | ~9 | *Romano Prodi wins Italian primaries.* names `inhabitant`, never `italian` |
| a pronoun or possessive named as a concept of its own | ~7 | *My stomach aches after meals.* names `my` |
| a clock time | 3 | *We'll eat at six o'clock.* names `o'clock` |
| `Let's` read as the verb "let" | 3 | *Let's climb to the top of this mountain.* names `let` |
| `what kind of X` | 2 | *What's your favorite kind of apple?* names `kind` |
| do-support in a question | 2 | *What do you wanna eat?* names `do` |
| `all of a sudden` | 2 | |

The nationality family is the sense ordering of 11-02: `teaching_sources.py` assembles a word's senses unsorted, so
NLTK's order governs and nouns come first. The mechanism is general to every word read as a modifier.

The joined-wrong bucket has **no** comparable families in it. The largest nameable family is 2.25 points of 400
sentences and the 95% bar is ~42 points away: the work is a measured tail, not one fix.

## The listener, on the store (`lyric_dev`, reads only)

`diagnostics/listener_on_store.py`, the same 400 sentences, memory warm.

| readings asked for | the reader's own first is right | the right reading is among those handed up | the listener took the right one | it overruled a right reader |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 33.2% | 33.2% | 33.2% | 0 |
| 6 | 33.5% | 35.0% | 33.5% | 0 |
| 25 | 33.5% | **37.8%** | 33.5% | 0 |

All ten learned worths are **+0.00** and every sentence traced *memory asked, knew nothing of any reading*: the
store holds **1** sentence record against 4,856 word records, and the listener's worths are learned from taught
lessons. Four of its five channels are therefore off, so the 4.6 points between 33.2% and 37.8% are available and
unclaimed and this store cannot say whether the listener could claim them. 35 of 400 say nothing at all. The 33.2%
baseline is not comparable with the files-only 54.2%; only within-run deltas are.

The trace found two defects in the number handed to the listener. `log(sure)` drops `_said_so`, which the reader
ranks by; fixing it to `Reading.ranked` changed nothing. The real one: the reader's sort key is lexicographic with
wholeness first (`_rank`), and a listener handed a scalar without that term re-decided wholeness on the words'
score — *My dog bit Tom.* as a thing called "dog bit" that Tom is, 12.5 nats surer by the words. `Grammar.readings`
now hands up only readings of the same wholeness as its first, and overrulings went from 4 to 0 at every width.
